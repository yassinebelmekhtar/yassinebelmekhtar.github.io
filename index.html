💡 What tab-display Does

Most people have a tablet lying around and a computer screen that always feels a little too small. tab-display connects the two. Your Mac sends its screen image to your tablet over a simple cable, and your tablet shows it as a smooth, responsive extra display. You can drag windows onto it, keep your chat app open there, or park a reference document beside your main work.

Because the connection is wired, the picture stays stable and the delay between moving something on your Mac and seeing it on the tablet is very small. That matters when you are drawing, writing by hand, or simply moving the mouse around. Wireless screen sharing often stutters, freezes, or drops out. A cable does not.

tab-display also understands pen and touch input. That means you can use a stylus directly on the tablet surface and have it register on your Mac, turning your tablet into a small drawing pad or a handy second control surface. Fingers work too, so tapping and scrolling feel natural.

🖱️ How to Arrange the Extra Screen

Once the tablet is connected, your computer treats it like a normal monitor. You can choose whether the tablet sits to the left or the right of your main screen, or even above or below it. Windows and apps can be dragged from one screen to the other as if they were always part of the same setup.

A common setup is to keep email, chat, and reference material on the tablet, and leave your main screen for the task you are actually working on. Another popular option is to place a drawing app on the tablet and use it as a pen surface while the main screen shows the rest of the project.

🛠️ If Something Does Not Work

Start with the cable. A cable that only charges a phone is not always good at carrying a video signal, and a weak cable is the single most common reason a second display stays black or keeps flickering. Try a different cable, and try a different port if your computer has one.

If the picture appears but feels slow, close any apps you are not using. Video encoding is work, and a busy computer has less room to do it. Disconnecting other screens and switching off background downloads also helps.

If pen input feels offset, open the display arrangement settings and check that the tablet screen is positioned where it physically sits on your desk. Matching the digital layout to the real layout fixes most pointing problems.

Before you try any of the above, make sure you are running the newest version from the download page. Updates often include small fixes that solve exactly the kind of issue you just hit.
